## C. Personality and psychology
**Canon summary** (M:108): "Meticulous, dry, guarded. Kind to young skippers. Correct procedure as armour."

**Five defining traits** `[BIBLE]` B01 §3:
1. **Meticulous.**
   - He checks the lamp, fuel and rotation in the same order every watch. If interrupted, he starts again rather than trust his memory.
   - His records are ruled and timed; corrections are marked, not overwritten.
2. **Dryly humorous.**
   - "At least we needn't carry the water upstairs."
   - Never laughs at his own jokes. Almost never jokes when another man is genuinely frightened.
3. **Guarded.**
   - Answers personal questions with practical information. Asked whether he remembers the wreck: "I was here."
   - Will discuss the weather that night before he discusses himself.
4. **Patient with the inexperienced.**
   - Gives a young skipper time to repeat a position without embarrassment. Never mistakes inexperience for stupidity.
   - When danger is close he gets *more precise, not louder*.
5. **Quietly controlling.**
   - Dislikes leaving a repair half-done. Reluctant to hand over a difficult task. Re-checks what others have checked.
   - His fear of error has become a habit of mistrust, **including mistrust of himself**.

**Motivation** (canon M:109: "Keep the light. Keep the peace. Keep his father's name.") `[BIBLE]` B01 §4:
- **Immediate:** keep the light working through the gale, answer calls correctly, protect vessels near the East Gut, and get through the watch without another death.
- **Conscious:** preserve the station's reputation and his father's memory without having to talk about 1953.
- **Deeper want:** to believe he can still be a good man without first admitting what he did as a frightened young one.
  - He doesn't seek forgiveness at the start, because that would mean admitting there is something to forgive.
  - His work is an argument he makes to himself each night: *The lamp is burning now. I am here now.*
  - The game tests whether that argument is enough.

**Internal conflict** `[CANON]` M:110: he loved his father, and he knows his father made him lie. "Every honest act betrays one of them."

**Fears** `[BIBLE]` B01 §5 (NARRATIVE.md:90 agrees):
1. **Becoming his father.** Not the drinking or the sleeping on duty (he keeps an especially strict watch against both), but choosing his own dignity over another person's life. He fears Iain's weakness was ordinary, and in the family, waiting for the right circumstances.
2. **Being found out.**
   - That would expose a skipper blamed on falsified evidence, and Callum's eighteen years of keeping it so.
   - He fears disgrace, losing his post, and the judgement of the people who trust his word.
   - More privately, he fears that if his father's reputation is stripped away, nothing will be left of their relationship except coercion.
   - **His worst fear is not that someone will call him a liar. It is that they will be right.**

**Flaws and contradictions:**
- Correct procedure is his armour.
- His kindness is real, and so is his evasion.
- His courage in the present is genuine. The question is whether he will apply it to a truth that threatens him personally (B01 §10).

**Emotional need:** to stop making his own competence stand in for an account of the past. He doesn't need forgiveness, which the game never promises.

## D. Voice and dialogue rules
**Canon voice** (M:188-196):

| Aspect | Rule |
|---|---|
| Sentence length | Short, often fragments |
| Formality | Correct procedure on air. Plain in his head |
| Vocabulary | Keeper's terms: exhibited, relit, bearing, the cage |
| Emotion shown | Through what he doesn't say, and through the machinery |
| Pattern | Repeats a fact to steady himself ("The light is burning") |
| Under pressure | Goes quieter and more procedural |
| Would never say | "I feel…", anything about forgiveness, a speech |

**Bible voice** `[BIBLE]` B01 §8:
- **Manner:** economical; local without theatrical dialect.
- **Feelings:** seldom explained directly. When upset he *shortens* his sentences.
- **Rhythm:**
  - usually two or three short clauses, sometimes one;
  - a small pause before anything personally difficult;
  - practical questions: "Where are you now?", not "How do you feel?";
  - under pressure his grammar stays orderly but his answers get more abrupt;
  - **his rare admissions often begin with a precise factual correction.**
- **Vocabulary:** "Aye", "right enough", "mind", "steady", "that'll do", "not yet", "I reckon", "you'll be wanting", "there's no need". A light Hebridean cadence: no phonetic spellings, no decorative Gaelic. An educated, locally rooted working man.
- **Verbal tics:**
  - "Right" when accepting something difficult;
  - "Aye" as agreement, or only as "I heard you";
  - when displeased, he **repeats a factual question** rather than making a personal accusation.
- **Radio:**
  - 1971 voice radio. Identifies the station, makes calls intelligible, reads back critical information, and keeps routine traffic apart from urgency and distress;
  - Channel 16 for distress, safety and calling, moving long exchanges to a working channel;
  - "over" when expecting a reply, "out" at the end. **Never "over and out."**
  - North Point is a lighthouse, not a Coastguard rescue coordination station. He relays and helps; he does not command a rescue or invent authority.
  - Typical: "Elspeth Ross, North Point Light. Say your position. Over." (In game he hails her ship, *Iona Star*; see FLAG-F10 on her rank.)
- **Never says:**
  - "I've got this", "We're all in this together", "You need to trust me", "Everything happens for a reason";
  - grand pronouncements about destiny, ghosts, guilt or the sea;
  - that he is haunted;
  - that a vessel *will* be safe.

## F. Narrative role and development
**Arc (canon):** silence → evasion → (the player's choice) truth, or the lie kept on purpose (M:115).

**Key scenes (canon):**
- ch1: the call;
- ch2: the archive;
- ch3: Morag's notes;
- ch4: the door;
- ch5: the letter;
- ch6: the dawn report (M:116).

**Beat by beat** `[BIBLE]` B01 §10, with the matching chapter:

1. **The ordinary watch (ch1): competence as protection.** Routine makes him look dependable. The player learns the room through his disciplined handling of the equipment. He corrects small deviations without comment.
2. **The first unsettling call (end of ch1 into ch2): a name returns.** His instinct is to verify rather than react. The player should hear the difference between professional caution and personal alarm.
3. **The past enters the room (ch2–3): records become evidence.** The 1953 log and Morag's notes show the official account is not neutral memory. Choices can be guarded or candid, but **the handwriting makes his involvement undeniable**.
4. **The knock (ch4): the man behind the record.** An unlit boat comes in under the light. He must face a living Alasdair Ross, not the dead skipper of the Inquiry.
5. **The confrontation (ch5): obedience examined.** He can defend his father's authority, or admit that obedience didn't make the entry true. His composure largely holds; his word choices get more revealing.
6. **The second failure (ch6): duty without evasion.** Elspeth's ship loses steering, the rotation jams again at 046°, and he and Ross keep the light turning by hand.
   - A living crew comes first, whatever has been said.
   - This is **not** a symbolic act standing in for rescue. The machinery and the maritime emergency must stay credible, urgent and practical.
7. **After the danger (ch6 dawn): the cost of speaking.** The radio and the log become the means by which a private decision enters the public record.

**Endings** `[COND]` (CANON_INDEX §7):
- **Truth:** he states plainly what he wrote, what was false, and what he knows about the timing. He asks no forgiveness and the game promises none. He accepts the exposure of his father's name rather than leaving it on another man.
- **Protective lie:** he keeps the account. He can still save the ship and do his duty well, and that is what makes it painful. Present courage doesn't undo past silence.

**Player choices that shape him** `[COND]`:
- `admitted_to_morag` (ch3);
- door choices (ch4: talk, identification, call Morag, check the log, open or keep shut);
- `confessed_to_ross` / `blamed_father` (ch5);
- `gave_letter` / `kept_letter` (ch5);
- `told_truth` / `kept_lie` (ch6).

**The essential arc is not coward-becomes-hero.** He is already capable of courage and kindness. The question is whether he will turn them on a truth that threatens him.

## G. Visual identity
`[BIBLE]` B01 §12 and the dossier. **Restrained naturalism**, not a weather-beaten sea captain.
- **Face:** a longish oval tending to rectangular. Broad, not heavy, forehead; defined cheekbones; a firm, slightly narrow jaw. More angular than gaunt. A straight nose with a modest irregularity at the bridge. Ordinary ears, perhaps a little prominent beneath the hair.
- **Age marks:**
  - looks 41, perhaps a few years older under harsh light;
  - fine horizontal forehead lines, creases at the outer eye corners, deeper nasolabial lines;
  - not elderly, not ill.
- **Skin:** fair to lightly ruddy; wind in the cheeks and around the nose; pores, slight unevenness, small dry patches. No scars or invented marks.
- **Hair:** brown (canon "dark", M:117), greying at the temples. Short, practical, combed without fuss. Slight recession at the hairline, not pronounced.
- **Shave:** clean-shaven; a late watch may leave a faint shadow (F06, resolved).
- **Eyes:** grey-blue or muted grey, moderately deep-set. Watchful, not piercing. He looks straight at equipment and charts, and is less comfortable holding a person's gaze over personal questions.
- **Expression at rest:**
  - lips closed, not pressed; brow faintly contracted in concentration; mouth corners neutral;
  - capable of a very small smile that **changes his eyes more than his mouth**.
- **At collar level:** a dark navy or charcoal wool guernsey over a pale, slightly worn collared shirt, the collar sitting naturally, perhaps unevenly. **No badges or uniform insignia.**

- **Service tools** `[BIBLE]`:

## I. Mystery and continuity
**What he knows** (`[CANON]` + `[BIBLE]` B01 §6):
- About 02:01 on 15.11.53 he woke and relit the lamp. The light had already failed, and he didn't see the whole failure sequence.
- He didn't wake in time to prevent the 01:58 grounding.
- At about 03:00 Iain dictated an entry placing the failure *after* the wreck and blaming Ross. Callum wrote it himself, knowingly.
- He has let it stand for eighteen years.
- The 046 tooth: he reported it in 1953 and 1961.

**What he suspects but cannot claim to have witnessed:**
- That Iain was drinking and failed to keep watch.
- He knows enough of Iain's behaviour that night to doubt the defence, but his own observations don't cover every minute.
- **An honest account must separate what he saw, what he wrote, and what he has concluded since.**

**What he doesn't know at the opening:**
- That Ross survived, lives as Sandy Morrison in Govan, and is coming to his door.
- That Iain's letter is in the bottom drawer (under the old Notices to Mariners, per §21 F2).
- Everything Morag's record could prove: the 01:52 Mayday is independent evidence of an unlit North Point before the grounding.

**What he hides:** that he wrote the entry; the half-confession of December 1953.

**What he unknowingly reveals, and when** `[BIBLE]` B01 §13:

| Moment | Reveal | What the player can read into it |
|---|---|---|
| Opening maintenance | Unusual attention to the rotation gear near 046° | A technical concern that predates tonight |
| First *Mary Ellen* call | His normally economical replies briefly become repetitive as he asks for identification | The name affects him personally |
| Old wreck discussed | Separates the timing of the failure from the timing of the grounding more carefully than an uninvolved keeper would | He knows more than the official account |
| The 1953 log | The hand matches his, numerals included | He didn't witness the document; he made it |
| Morag's record | Hesitates over 01:52, which precedes the wreck | The official chronology can't be reconciled with independent radio evidence |
| The Visitor arrives | Can't say where his own knowledge ends and his father's account begins | His silence is partly shame, partly an inherited version of events |
| Jam at 046° | Recognises the failure pattern and reacts quickly | The technical cause of 1953 is not the Visitor's invention |
| Final radio or log | Corrects the record, or leaves it | The mystery ends as an ethical decision, not just a discovery of facts |

## J. Development constraints
- **Player-character principle** `[BIBLE]` B01 §13:
  - Callum must never feel like a different person hidden behind the player's back. His guilt comes out through actions, objects, partial admissions, and the tension between his spoken choices and the evidence in the room.
  - A player who always chooses protective dialogue should still see his kindness; one who chooses honest dialogue should still see his fear. **Both versions are recognisably the same man.**
  - "The truth does not change his past. It changes whether he is willing to make someone else carry it any longer."
- **Writers:**
  - Keep him economical. No speeches, no "I feel…".
  - Admissions start with a factual correction.
  - Never let him promise safety or speak with Coastguard authority.
  - His thoughts in ch2 are evasive, not ignorant.
- **Knowledge:** never let him know Ross is alive before ch4–5, or the letter's text before ch5.
- **3D and portrait artists:** follow §G: guernsey and shirt, no tunic (F06 resolved). No sea-captain clichés, no invented scars or insignia.
- **Animators and level designers:**
  - ordered checking; papers squared to the desk edge;
  - the gear inspected under the same light;
  - books closed gently; no cup on the log.
- **Document and UI artists:** the handwriting must match across 1953 and 1971 (FLAG-F15). Document wording comes only from CANON_INDEX §6.
- **AI agents:** don't treat the dossier image's log texts as canon (FLAG-F03). Don't invent new history to fill gaps; raise a G-flag instead.
