**Summary** `[BIBLE]` B01 §1: a precise, stubborn radio officer and Callum's oldest friend, whose calm voice has guided frightened mariners through emergencies for many years. She kept a pencilled record of the 1953 Mayday that contradicts the official account. Tonight she has to decide whether protecting Callum means sparing him again, or finally demanding an answer.

**Central contradiction** `[BIBLE]`: she trusts written facts more than memory, yet for eighteen years she has questioned the one piece of evidence she can't bring herself to discard.

## C. Personality and psychology
**Canon summary** (M:124): "Crisp and correct on air. Warm, teasing and stubborn off procedure after midnight."

**Five defining traits** `[BIBLE]` B01 §3:
1. **Precise.**
   - Asks for an uncertain bearing again before entering it. Reads figures back slowly.
   - Never substitutes a plausible guess for a missing detail. Her pencil waits until she has enough to write.
2. **Warm beneath formality.**
   - After midnight, when traffic is light, she softens. She remembers who has worked too long and who has a young family ashore.
   - With Callum she may ask whether he has eaten, but only after the business is finished.
3. **Stubborn.**
   - Won't discard the 1953 notes, despite years with no prospect of challenging the Inquiry.
   - Challenged about their accuracy, she **checks them again** rather than arguing from pride.
4. **Emotionally disciplined.**
   - Donald's death taught her the practical cruelty of waiting for information.
   - In emergencies she avoids speculation, even when asked for reassurance she can't give.
5. **Quietly confrontational.**
   - Rarely raises her voice. She repeats a question until it is answered or plainly refused.
   - Her silences can be more uncomfortable than anger.

**Motivation:**
- **Canon** (M:125): "A quiet watch. And the one Mayday she has never been able to put down."
- `[BIBLE]` B01 §4:
  - **Immediate:** reliable contact, vessel positions, and coordinating the Coastguard response around North Point. When the *Iona Star* loses steering: get position and condition, alert and coordinate rescue resources through procedure, and keep the channel orderly.
  - **Personal:** why has the name *Mary Ellen* come back, and do tonight's events expose the flaw in the 1953 account?
  - **Deeper want: to stop treating loyalty and truth as opposing duties.**
    - She couldn't bring Donald home, or the seven men of the *Mary Ellen*, or make the Inquiry agree with what she wrote.
    - She wants her observations to have *mattered*. Not for vindication, but because a man was blamed, a family shamed, and seven families left with an account she has never reconciled with her record.
    - She needs to believe that speaking at the right time can still do good.

**Fear** (canon M:126: "that she was wrong about the time, or that she was right") `[BIBLE]` B01 §5:
- **If she was wrong** (clock, hearing, recording), challenging the Inquiry would do the family a fresh injustice. Trusting herself professionally makes the possibility of error *more* serious, not less.
- **If she was right** (an unlit North Point at 01:52, six minutes before the grounding), the official chronology is false. Then Callum has either known something essential or lived beside a lie without asking what she has asked herself for eighteen years.
- She fears losing him, and fears finding that their friendship depended on her not knowing.
- **The second fear is worse because it demands action.**

**Internal conflict** `[CANON]` M:126: her guilt at staying silent in 1954.

## D. Voice and dialogue rules
**Canon voice** (M:188-196):

| Aspect | Rule |
|---|---|
| Sentence length | Medium on air, longer off it |
| Formality | Full procedure on air. First names slip out under stress |
| Vocabulary | Stornoway radio-room terms: roger, wait one, received and logged |
| Emotion shown | Through teasing, then through first names |
| Pattern | "Aye." "Right." Long pauses (`...`) |
| Under pressure | Drops procedure: "Callum! Answer me!" |
| Would never say | Anything unkind about the dead |

**Bible voice** `[BIBLE]` B01 §8. Two registers, formal radio and quieter personal, and the contrast between them is how she is recognised.
- **On air:** short, complete sentences. Critical words and numbers repeated when necessary. No speculation on air.
  - The bible's "short, complete" sits with the canon's "medium on air". Read it as: complete and economical, never chatty.
- **Off procedure** (late, with Callum): slightly longer sentences, warmer wit. Questions about food, sleep and weather are how she shows care.
- **First names when worried.** "North Point Light" becomes "Callum"; "*Iona Star*" or "Ross" becomes "Elspeth". It must be rare enough to notice. In ch2 the player hears her say "Callum" earlier than usual.
- **Vocabulary:** "Aye", "Right then", "That's understood", "Say again", "Very well", "Mind yourself", "I'll need that once more", "You're not answering me". Nothing flowery about the sea. Her language comes from work, family and living with poor weather.
- **Radio:**
  - identifies her station, confirms essentials, keeps distress traffic clear;
  - in 1971, Channel 16 for distress and calling, with working channels arranged as needed;
  - no modern digital procedures;
  - typical: "North Point Light, Stornoway Coastguard. Say again your last message. Over."
  - **In a real emergency she doesn't abandon procedure for personal history.** If Callum starts a long confession while the *Iona Star* is in danger, she directs him back to the rescue.
- **Period note:** the 1953 Mayday must not be shown as a modern Channel 16 or digital call. Coastguard VHF was expanding through the 1960s (B01 cites commsmuseum.co.uk).
- **Tics:**
  - a difficult personal question may begin with "Now";
  - unconvinced, she says "Is that so?", without sarcasm;
  - **her most revealing habit is asking for the same figure twice.**
- **Never says:**
  - "Trust your instincts", "We'll figure this out together", "I know exactly how you feel";
  - a speech about the dead demanding justice;
  - she never calls herself a widow to win an argument.

## I. Mystery and continuity
- **She knows** (`[CANON]` / `[BIBLE]` B01 §6):
  - she took the Mayday at 01:52 and wrote down "no light";
  - the official chronology puts the failure *after* the 01:58 wreck;
  - Callum was at the station that night, and Iain was principal keeper.
- **She doesn't initially know:**
  - that Iain dictated a false entry;
  - that Callum physically wrote it;
  - that Ross survived. She learns it is Ross in ch5 (M:276; FLAG-F18).
- **What she hasn't said:**
  - she has never been fully candid with Callum about how often she has gone back over those times;
  - friendship made her hesitant to demand an explanation, and professional uncertainty gave her a reason to put it off.
- **Her silence is not complicity.** She suspects the account is wrong; she doesn't know how the lie was made.
- **Knowledge boundaries by chapter** (M:276):

  | Ch1 | Ch2 | Ch3 | Ch4 | Ch5 | Ch6 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | the anniversary | the caller knew the words; *(optional)* the sister boat went to "Mr Morrison, Govan" | the time gap, plus whatever Callum admits | a man at the door (if called) | it's Ross | what Callum reports |

- **Evidential role:** her 01:52 "no light" is independent radio evidence that North Point was dark before the 01:58 grounding. It contradicts the false 02:10 entry by 18 minutes. When she produces it in ch3, the mystery shifts from *what happened* to *whether Callum will say it* `[IMG]` §13.

## J. Development constraints
- **Writers:**
  - Two registers; the switch to first names is rare and noticeable.
  - She asks for a figure twice.
  - In an emergency, procedure comes before history.
  - No forgiveness speech; a small acknowledgement is enough.
  - Nothing unkind about the dead. She never plays the widow.
- **Knowledge:** she must not know Ross is alive before ch5, or that Callum wrote the entry before he admits it (ch3, optional) or it comes out in ch5–6.
- **Evidence handling:** she never claims her notes prove *everything*. They prove the time and the phrase.
- **Period:** keep "MRSC" out of game text until verified (FLAG-F14). The 1953 call was not a modern Channel 16 call.
- **3D and portrait:** dark brown hair greying at the temples, grey-green eyes, no glasses, no pin (F16 resolved). She is not glamorous: a composed, attentive working woman of 44.
- **Props:** the notebook is pencil, a private hand. Never ink, never an official form.
