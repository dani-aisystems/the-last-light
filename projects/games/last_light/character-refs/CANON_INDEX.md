**Hard rules:**
- **The player learns Callum wrote the entry (ch2) before Callum says so.** Callum's thoughts are evasive, not ignorant (M:76).
- **Ross's identity:** no name, portrait, card, history entry, accessibility text or saved metadata may reveal "Alasdair Ross" before the `knows_name` flag. Until then he is "The man at the door" (speakers.gd). He is **never** shown in a full portrait (FLAG-F12, Daniel's rule).
- **Morag** doesn't know that Iain dictated the entry or that Callum wrote it until Callum admits it (ch3, optional) or the letter or Ross brings it out. She does not know Ross is alive until ch5.
- **Elspeth** doesn't know her father is alive until ch6, and only on the reunion path.
- **Nothing is supernatural.** Each eerie event has a cause that Ross admits in ch5: the carrier, the calls, the 046 echo, the dead 16, the jam (M:82-90). The "ghost boat" is island legend only.

## 5. Order of discoveries (the mystery's control)
1. **Ch1:** Callum knows something. He sat looking at the date. "It's the fourteenth." Rory's grandfather's ghost story.
2. **Ch2:** Callum wrote the 1953 entry (the archive, two logs, the same hand).
3. **Ch3:** the entry is false. Morag's notebook has 01:52 and "no light"; the log has the failure at 02:10.
4. **Ch4:** the caller was there. He knows the gear race, the bottle, the song. SY 214.
5. **Ch5:** Ross explains the calls; Iain's letter explains why.
6. **Ch6:** what Callum does about it is the player's decision.

## 8. Dialogue and period rules (all characters)
- **Language:**
  - 1971 Hebridean English with a light touch of Scots (aye, wee, och, skelp, kirk, lass);
  - no phonetic accent spellings;
  - no Gaelic inserted for atmosphere;
  - no modern slang (M:198-202; B01 Callum §8).
- **Nobody explains** what the listener already knows, except as radio procedure.
- **Cryptic lines** come only from the *Mary Ellen* caller in ch1–2, and Ross explains them in ch5.
- **Radio procedure in 1971:**
  - voice radio only, nothing digital;
  - Channel 16 is for distress, safety and calling; long exchanges move to a working channel;
  - "over" when expecting a reply, "out" to end the exchange, **never "over and out"**;
  - read back critical figures;
  - North Point is a lighthouse, not a rescue coordination centre. Callum relays and assists, and the Coastguard coordinates (B01 Callum §8, Morag §8).
- **The 1953 Mayday** must not be shown as a modern Channel 16 or digital call (B01 Morag §8).
- **Voice table:** M:186-196, repeated in each PROFILE §D.
- **Nothing supernatural:** no ghosts, no destiny speeches. The "ghost boat" is island legend only (Rory, Elspeth). Ross may use "ghost" dryly about himself (F21).
- **Unverified period terms:** "MRSC" is not used; say "HM Coastguard, Stornoway" (F14).
- **The death count** (F19):
  - eight aboard the *Mary Ellen*;
  - **officially seven lost, the skipper among them**;
  - **in truth six died**: Ross and Kenny Bain survived.

  Characters who believe Ross drowned say "seven".
