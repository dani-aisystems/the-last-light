**Summary** `[IMG]` §1: the long-serving principal keeper of North Point, a competent and respected man whose life was overturned by grief, drink and a moment of negligence that cost seven men (officially; six in truth, F19) in 1953. He never appears in person. His presence lives on through the records he kept, a photograph on the wall, and a letter he never sent to his son.

## B. Canonical background
**The man before his wife died** `[IMG]` §2:
- A capable, steady keeper and a respected figure on the island. Practical, disciplined and skilled with machinery: the sort of man who could keep a light running through hard weather with few complaints and less conversation.
- He took pride in the station and its reputation, kept thorough records, and expected the same care from those under him.
- At home he was not a man for sentiment, but he was a reliable husband and father. His wife softened the edges of his reserve, and their home was known for its quiet order.
- With Callum he could be strict, sometimes distant. Yet he taught by example: how to maintain the lamp, read the weather, and value a job well done.
- Canon adds: he could show affection practically (mending boots, teaching a knot) but found spoken affection difficult (Callum B01 §2). He said Callum "wrote like a clerk" (ch1:137).

**The night of 14–15 November 1953, from Iain's side** (`[IMG]` §3 reconciled with `[CANON]`):
- **23:30:** takes the middle watch in poor condition, with the bottle. Not openly drunk, but not fit for a long, alert watch.
- **≈01:40:** the rotation stalls at **046°** on the worn tooth: the fault he reported in 1949.
- **≈01:50:** the lamp, no longer turning and untended, starves and goes out. Overcome by drink and exhaustion, **Iain is asleep in the watch-room chair** and misses the failure.
- **01:52:** the *Mary Ellen*'s Mayday. **He half-hears the wireless in his sleep and takes it for a dream** (canon letter; F20).
- **01:58:** the *Mary Ellen* strikes the East Gut.
- **02:01:** Callum wakes and relights. Too late.
- **≈03:00:** faced with what has happened, Iain makes the decision that defines the rest of his life. He dictates a false entry that moves the failure to after the wreck and shifts the blame to Ross, and has Callum write it in his own hand, knowing the young man's loyalty and obedience. He signs it "I. Mackay, P.K." (written in Callum's hand, I02).
- **In doing so** he protects his own name and the station's reputation, at the cost of a man's good name and his own son's peace.

## C. Personality and psychology
**What he represents** `[IMG]` §5:
- duty and pride in the lighthouse service;
- the destructive weight of grief and silence;
- the choice to protect his name rather than face the truth;
- a father both respected and feared, still present in Callum's life long after his death.

**Traits** (drawn from the dossier and canon):
- competent and disciplined;
- reserved, unsentimental; proud of the station;
- a tidy man ("Iain was a tidy man. Look in the bottom drawer." ch5:78);
- capable of real friendship (Ross) and of real cowardice.

**In his own words** (canon letter): "I was asleep in this chair with the bottle … and the light was out, and I could not face it." "Ross was a better man than me, and he was my friend." "If you ever have the courage I did not, put it right."

**Contradiction:** he loved his son and made him carry his lie. He wrote the confession, but left it unsent in a drawer.

## D. Voice and dialogue rules
Iain has no spoken lines. His voice exists only in writing, and in others' memories of him.
- **The canon voice** is the Christmas 1957 letter (CANON_INDEX §6, letter_1957). It is short, plain and unadorned:
  - he names his failure directly;
  - he gives no excuses beyond "I could not face it";
  - he addresses his son as "Callum" and signs "Your father, Iain Mackay".
- **The log voice:** official and clipped ("Light extinguished approx. 2 min (mantle failure). Relit 02.12."). That is his phrasing in Callum's hand.
- **The note voice:** terse, technical, initialled ("GEAR RACE - worn tooth at 046°. Sticks in heavy weather.").
- **Remembered speech:** "Father always said I wrote like a clerk" (ch1:137).
- **Never:** sentimental flourishes, self-pity at length, or blaming Callum.

**The dossier's alternative letter is a voice study, NOT canon** (F04). Transcribed from the image; [?] marks illegible words.

> North Point Light / 3 March 1958
>
> Callum,
>
> I have put this off too long, and now I do not know that I shall have another chance to say it to you in person. So I set it down, if only to clear my own mind.
>
> You have served the light well, and I am proud of the man you have become. There is a steadiness in you I did not always have, and I know the station is in safe hands with you.
>
> There is something else I must say. About the night of the Mary Ellen. You know what we wrote in the book, and you know it was not the [whole?] truth. I was not [fit for?] watch. I had been drinking, and I slept. The light failed, and men died for it. I have lived with that a long time.
>
> I told myself I did it to protect your service, and you too. Perhaps there was some truth in that. But it was also to save my own name. That is a poor excuse, and I know it. I am sorry for the burden I put on you. A man should not make his son keep a lie.
>
> I do not know what is right for you to do now. I cannot ask you to set the record straight, nor can I ask you to keep it as it stands. That is for you to judge, when the time comes. Only remember this — a light is of no use if it does not show the truth.
>
> Your father, / Iain

**Why it isn't canon:**
- its date and length conflict with the shipped Christmas 1957 letter;
- it omits "the girl at Stornoway … ten minutes to two", which is evidence;
- in March 1958 Callum was posted elsewhere, so "the station is in safe hands with you" doesn't fit.

Its phrase *"a light is of no use if it does not show the truth"* captures Iain's values, and may be used as a writer's touchstone, never as a quotation.

## G. Visual identity

- **Facial hair:** a **full moustache**.
- **Hair:** dark, wavy, greying, swept back.
- **In May 1952 (aged 48):** upright, weathered, with the quiet authority of a man at his post.
- **Clothing:** a heavy knitted roll-neck or guernsey. In the photograph, a keeper's cap and heavy coat (dossier wall-photo panel), which suits an outdoor station portrait. The canon photo shows him **with Callum, his arm round him**.

## I. Mystery and continuity
- **What he knew:**
  - the tooth was worn (he reported it in 1949);
  - he was asleep and drunk on watch;
  - the light failed before the wreck;
  - "the girl at Stornoway had it right";
  - Ross was his friend and was blamed falsely.
- **What he did:**
  - dictated the lie;
  - stood by it at the Inquiry;
  - wrote the truth in December 1957 but never sent it;
  - told Kenny it was in the desk.
- **What he never knew:** that Ross survived.
- **Continuity rules:**
  - The letter is canon text. Never paraphrase it in a document.
  - Iain heard the wireless "in my sleep" (F20).
  - He was principal keeper of North Point 1946–58.
  - He died in March 1958 and is buried in Tarbert.

## J. Development constraints
- **Writers:**
  - Iain never speaks or appears.
  - Characters remember him truthfully: competent, proud, grieving, drinking, afraid.
  - He is not a villain. The game shows the weight of grief that drove the lie, and does not excuse the lie.
- **Document artists:**
  - his hand is copperplate (envelope, his own notes);
  - the 1953 entry and its signature row are in Callum's hand.
- **3D:** use the dossier likeness (moustache, wavy greying hair, guernsey) for the 1952 photograph.
- **AI agents:**
  - do not quote the dossier's "3 March 1958" letter as Iain's letter;
  - do not use the dossier's solo 1940s photo as the wall photograph.
