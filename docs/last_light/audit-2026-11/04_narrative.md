### 7.2 Pacing problems found
- Chapter 1 jumps from 18:50 to 23:25 with nothing between.
- Hector arrives cold; Elspeth's first line is also her reveal.
- Chapter 3 stacks two rescues, Elspeth, the notebook and a blackout within seconds of each other.
- Chapter 5 gives the wreck, Pabbay, the Inquiry, the library, the calls and Kenny in one conversation.
- Four beats wait on the player with nothing else happening.

### 7.3 Balanced restructure
**Chapter 1, a working evening (fills 18:50 to 23:25, about +12 minutes).**
- 19:10 Gillies on the *Sula*: Thursday's relief; the 046 tooth is "down for the next refit". Teaches placing a call.
- 20:00 the neighbouring keeper's nightly schedule: weather exchanged, a word about Callum's father.
- 21:00 routine duties: read the barometer, write the weather line, wind the standby clockwork, put the kettle on. The "Radio check 2100" on the bezel card now happens (resolves that conflict).
- 21:40 Hector heard on 16 reporting to Stornoway, so his voice is known before chapter 2.
- The first teleprinter sheet arrives and becomes readable.

**Chapter 2 (about +5 minutes).** The *Iona Star* is overheard giving a routine passage report, a calm unnamed mate's voice. Dr Marsh's one exchange. The archive beat gains a nudge from the neighbour's earlier remark about "your father's books".

**Chapter 3 (about +8 minutes).** Opens with the fuel run. Visser and the *Hendrika* after both rescues are settled. The neighbouring keeper's schedule is missed: his station has gone dark, heard as an empty carrier. Six to eight seconds of air added between the stacked beats.

**Chapter 4.** Unchanged in story; restaged (section 6); generator lock begins.

**Chapter 5 (same content, three movements).** (1) The wreck and Pabbay at the door. (2) The player does something: boils the kettle and hands him a cup, the cup he sets down at dawn. Then the library and the Board. (3) The calls and Kenny, then the drawer. The sediment-bowl step sits between the letter and the Mayday.

**Chapter 6.** Unchanged. Visser, if saved, is heard thanking Stornoway in the background at dawn.

**Estimated effect:** about +170 lines, +25 minutes. No ending, reveal or canon fact changes.

### 7.7 Dialogue system
- All lines advance on reading time: 1.0 s + 0.06 s per character (today's formula), minimum 2.8 s, plus 0.7 s when the speaker changes and 0.4 s after a full stop ending a message.
- A "Reading time" slider in Options (0.75x to 3x), so slower readers get longer; no skip. The maximum time per message rises from 9 s to about 12 s.
- Pause works; time under a document, fade or pause does not count.
- History (H) unchanged. Endings: panels and lines advance by themselves only, scaled by the reading-time setting. Credits after an ending lose the Space speed-up; Esc still leaves them, since they carry no story.
- The `dialogue_next` action is removed from the input map once nothing reads it.
- Bots and tests keep their fast path (`bot_line_s`), behind the debug gate.

**Save compatibility (part of Stage A).** Before any story file is edited, a one-off headless run records a save at every beat of the current build as test fixtures. Then: save version 2 with a migration step and a one-time backup of the old autosave; beats addressed by id, not index, with an alias table for renames; a backfill list for new flags. A permanent `save_compat_test` proves every old fixture loads onto the right beat and plays to the end.

### 7.8 Sound
- **Radio:** no voice exists, so intelligibility is text pacing and static level. Static dips 6 dB while a message is on screen; squelch and typing blips stay.
- **Ross:** six to eight cough takes from one older male voice (CC0, after Daniel's OK), never the same take twice in a row, played from the stair positions with a low-pass for the closed door and a stairwell reverb send; breaths between lines.
- **Handling sounds to add (recorded CC0 where a synth would sound false, else synth):** boots on concrete, chequer plate and iron treads; door wheel, dogs and hinge; paper slide and document open/close (12 standing test warnings); telephone lift and dead line; switch and breaker; hand pump; kettle; sediment-bowl drain; teleprinter.
- **Mix:** a Room reverb send on SFX; music ducks 8 dB under any message; the existing ambience duck is kept for silences.
- Daniel judges all audio by ear from listening files; I never play audio.

## 17. Document 4 in full: narrative expansion proposal

### 17.1 Canon in brief (verified against the story files)
- **Player:** Callum Mackay, 41, principal keeper since 1962, born Tarbert 1930, assistant under his father here 1950 to 1955. He wrote the false 1953 log entry in his own hand at his father's order.
- **Night:** Sunday 14 to Monday 15 November 1971, the eighteenth anniversary of the *Mary Ellen*.
- **Cast:** Morag Fraser (44, Stornoway Coastguard, took the 1953 Mayday); Rory Bain (22, *Kittiwake*, Kenny Bain's grandson); Hector Macleod (about 68, schooner *Gannet*); Elspeth Ross (29, mate of MV *Iona Star*, Alasdair's daughter); Alasdair "Sandy" Ross (64, master of the *Mary Ellen*, presumed drowned); Iain Mackay (1904 to 1958, present only through objects).
- **The stair:** it is Alasdair Ross who climbs it. He does not speak until the door.
- **Left unresolved on purpose:** Mrs Ross's name and fate; Callum's mother's name; what Iain was thinking; what Callum truly saw that night; what Rory is told afterwards; Ross's fate in the lie endings.
- **Language:** English only. One Gaelic song title.

### 17.5 Arcs the additions serve

### 17.7 Narrative risks
- Four new voices can crowd a small cast: each is capped at one or two short scenes and none speaks after chapter 3 except in the background.
- Routine traffic can become filler: each routine call teaches a control or plants a later detail, listed in a table in the script document.
- The longer generator outage changes chapter 5's mood: it is dark, on battery, for Ross's whole account. This is a strength if the lighting holds; checked at the Stage R capture.
- Period claims not yet verified: inter-station radio schedules; keepers' weather reporting routine; the NLB motto wording; seismic survey vessels west of the Hebrides in 1971; the Flannan Isles details. Each is checked against a named source or labelled as invention before it ships.
